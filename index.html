The discovery

What Rutherford Found

"Rutherford's gold foil experiment demonstrated that atoms are mostly empty space, with nearly all of their mass and positive charge concentrated in a tiny, dense nucleus."

This directly contradicted Thomson's plum pudding model, which predicted that alpha particles should pass through with only slight, gentle deflections — never a large-angle or backward scatter. The actual investigation was carried out by Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden, working under Rutherford's direction; Rutherford published the nuclear model in 1911.
